Diagnosing an SEO Paradox: Rising Visibility, Falling Clicks

Search visibility went up. Website clicks didn't grow at the same rate. Here's how I found out why — using cross-referenced data and live verification, not just a dashboard average.

ClientElite IIT Coaching
RoleDigital Marketing Manager
PeriodApril – September 2026
ToolsGoogle Search Console, GA4, Google Business Profile, Live SERP Audits

The Paradox, In Plain Terms

You don't need to know SEO to understand what happened here — just three numbers.

Between April and August, Elite IIT's website showed up in Google search results 37.9% more often. That's a good sign — more people searching for coaching-related terms were seeing the site. But the number of people who actually clicked through to the website only grew 10.5% — and the "click rate" (the percentage of people who saw the listing and clicked it) actually fell by 20%.

In other words: more people were seeing Elite IIT in search results, but a smaller share of them were clicking through. On the surface, that looks like the SEO work wasn't working. It took four separate investigations to find out that wasn't the real story.

Investigation 1 — Google's AI Answers Are Eating Clicks

In Plain English

Google now sometimes shows an AI-written summary answer at the very top of search results, above the normal list of websites — you've probably seen this yourself when searching something. If Elite IIT's content gets used as a source for that AI summary, the person searching often gets their answer right there and never actually clicks through to the website — even though Google is quoting Elite IIT's content. This is a genuinely new, industry-wide shift in how search works — not something specific to this account.

What I Did to Confirm It

1

Pulled a report from Google showing which of Elite IIT's pages had been used as a source in these AI-generated answers during August — found this happened 10,986 times, accounting for 14.7% of all the month's search appearances.

2

Compared the 8 pages used most often in these AI answers against the pages that had lost the most clicks between April and August.

3

Found every single one of those 8 pages had lost clicks — zero exceptions. That's a strong, consistent pattern, not a coincidence.

4

Calculated that this explains roughly 60% of the total click shortfall across the whole site — the single biggest factor behind the paradox.

Investigation 2 — Ruling Out a Tempting Wrong Answer

A natural next guess: maybe the falling click rate itself caused Google to rank pages lower, which would explain both problems as one thing. I checked Google's own official guidance on this — and Google has publicly stated that click rate is not something they use to decide rankings. So a page losing both clicks and position needed a separate explanation, not one causing the other. Ruling this out prevented chasing the wrong fix.

Investigation 3 — Where the Visibility Landed Also Changed

In Plain English

Not all search positions are equal. Being one of the top 3 results gets far more clicks than being 4th–10th, even if both technically "rank." Over this period, a smaller share of Elite IIT's visibility was landing in that valuable top-3 spot, and more was landing in the lower 4-10 range — like moving from a storefront on the main street to one a block further back. Same visibility on paper, fewer people walking in.

Investigation 4 — Checking the Positives Were Real Too

It's easy to only look for problems once you're diagnosing a decline. I also directly re-checked whether an earlier concern — a dip in branded search terms like "Elite IIT," "Elite IIT Malleshwaram," and similar — was still an active issue.

✓

Opened an incognito browser window (so no personal search history could skew results) and manually searched each branded term live on September 7. Every one of them had recovered to the #1 position. The dip visible in the monthly average was a temporary blip, not an ongoing problem — confirmed by checking reality directly, not just trusting a chart.

A Related Finding — Why Some Branches Rank Better Locally

While investigating search performance, a parallel question came up: why do some of the 4 branches consistently outrank others on Google Maps and local search? Two data-backed answers came out of a separate but related audit:

  • How often new reviews come in matters more than the star rating itself. A competitive check of top-ranking coaching institutes' Google Business Profiles showed that one branch (Malleshwaram) outranks even higher-rated competitors simply by generating new reviews consistently — about 2 a day.
  • Keeping a Google Business Profile's information complete matters too. Branches that filled out nearly all available service listings (83 of 85) improved their ranking; one branch that had only filled out 15 of 85 became the weakest performer. That branch's gap wasn't neglect — a busy month with a webinar launch and ad campaign left almost no time for this specific task, which is worth knowing so it doesn't happen again.

Why This Matters Going Forward

This is part of a broader shift the whole search industry is adjusting to — sometimes called AEO (Answer Engine Optimization). As AI-generated answers become more common in search results, ranking #1 doesn't guarantee a click the way it used to. The skill that matters increasingly is understanding why a number moved, not just reporting that it did — and having a repeatable way to check: compare the data, verify it live, rule out the tempting wrong answer, and look at the operational side (like GBP completeness) alongside the algorithmic side.

The bottom line: the SEO work was not failing. Growth was real (37.9% more visibility, 55% of tracked keywords improved). The click-rate dip had two explainable, mostly external causes — and the branded-term "problem" wasn't a problem at all once checked properly.
