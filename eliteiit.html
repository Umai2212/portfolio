Full-Stack Digital Marketing for Elite IIT Coaching

Joined as sole digital marketing manager for a 4-branch coaching institute in Bangalore — audited a broken setup, rebuilt paid, organic, and local search from the ground up, and ran it end-to-end for 4+ months across Meta Ads, Google Ads, SEO, and Google Business Profile.

ClientElite IIT Coaching, Bangalore
RoleDigital Marketing Manager
PeriodMay 2026 – Present
ChannelsMeta Ads · Google Ads · SEO · GBP · WordPress

The Situation

Elite IIT runs 4 branches across Bangalore — Yelahanka, Jayanagar, Koramangala, and Malleshwaram — offering JEE, NEET, and Class 3–12 tuition. They had been using freelancers and hired a full-time manager for the first time.

When I joined: Meta Ads running with no qualification filters and an inflated lead count, Google Ads conversion tracking broken since launch, GBP profiles at position 16+ city-wide, the highest-selling product (Class 3–12 tuition) with zero digital presence, and an internal narrative that performance had dropped — based on numbers that turned out to be misleading.

What I Walked Into

  • 18-month Meta Ads average CPL of ₹65.91 — inflated by unqualified leads
  • Facebook page restricted — 97% delivery on Instagram only
  • Google Ads showing 14 form submissions with zero emails received — tracking broken since launch
  • NEET page at rank ~24 with 2,200 words and 2 organic visits/month
  • GBP profiles unoptimised — Class 3–12 tuition missing across all 4 branches
  • No leads tracking, no reporting infrastructure
  • Website loading in 7 seconds — 1,160 impressions/day but only 14 clicks

What Was Expected

  • Lower CPL than the previous freelancer's ₹53.37
  • More qualified leads reaching the counseling team
  • Local SEO visibility across all 4 branches
  • Proper tracking and reporting with real numbers
  • A digital marketing function that runs independently

Meta Ads

The previous setup had a single NEET campaign with no segmentation and no qualification layer. Internal comparisons had also been mixing Navodaya, School Tuition, and job-applicant leads into one "NEET leads" bucket — the comparison being used internally was inaccurate.

I restructured the account into separate campaigns per product, geo-targeted per branch, with a WhatsApp qualification filter screening leads before they reached the counseling team — then ran that approach at increasing scale over the following months.

What I Did

  • Restructured from 1 generic campaign into segmented campaigns per product (NEET, Class 6–12, Navodaya, Dropper)
  • Set up geo-targeting per branch — Yelahanka, Koramangala, Jayanagar, Malleshwaram
  • Built a WhatsApp qualification filter — screening questions before a lead reaches a counselor
  • Changed age targeting from 28–45 to 18–45 to capture students directly alongside parents
  • Built audience segments: website visitors, interest-based (NEET, JEE, coaching), lookalikes
  • Launched the NEET Crash Course campaign the same day the re-exam was announced — in-house copy, 4 creatives approved, landing page live within the week, 104 leads generated
  • Sustained and scaled the qualification-first approach over 4 months, not just the launch month

Google Ads

Tracking Fix

Rakesh flagged at 8:55 PM on June 10 that Google Ads was showing 14 form submissions but no emails had been received. I responded at 5:01 AM June 11 and had the root cause diagnosed and fixed by 12:04 PM — 15 hours total.

Root Cause: The lead form asset was collecting submissions but no conversion event was firing and email notifications had never been configured. The ads were spending with no optimisation signal going back to Google. This had been broken since the account launched — nobody had caught it until the flag came in.

  • Implemented Thank You page redirect as the conversion signal
  • Configured Google Tag Manager to fire the conversion event on thank you page load
  • Set test campaign at ₹200/day to verify end-to-end before scaling
  • Conversion tracking confirmed live — Google Ads now has a proper optimisation signal

Ongoing Management

Beyond the fix, Google Ads ran continuously on Search for Navodaya/Sainik/RMS programs at roughly ₹130 CPC with a 50–80% lead-to-enrollment rate reported by the counseling team — a materially higher conversion rate than Meta's school-tuition funnel (20–30%), consistent with Search capturing higher-intent queries.

SEO — From Invisible to Compounding Growth

The NEET page was getting 2 organic visits a month at rank ~24 in May. Rebuilding it was the start of a broader SEO effort that ran continuously through September.

What Was Rebuilt

  • NEET page: grew from 2,200 to 4,800 words, hub-and-spoke structure, FAQ schema, internal linking
  • Malleshwaram: built a dedicated location page, optimised GBP — moved to #2 Search / #4 Maps within a month, from no ranking at all
  • Site speed: identified a 7-second page load time as the cause of high impressions (1,160/day) with almost no clicks (14) — a technical issue independent of the content work
  • Schema markup: implemented Course schema across course pages — improves both bot content-understanding and rich-result eligibility (ratings, FAQs, price) in search
  • Technical audits: ran site crawls via Screaming Frog to audit internal linking structure; corrected meta tags on 42 of 43 pages flagged

The Structural Fix — Site Architecture

The site had generic location pages ("Coaching in Yelahanka") and generic course pages ("NEET Coaching in Bangalore") — but was missing the intersection of the two. That single gap explained most of the "not ranking" results across School Tuition, Navodaya/Sainik/RMS, and CLAT programs. Identified independently across two separate audits a month apart, this became the top priority on the content roadmap — reframed as a content problem, not a ranking problem.

What Drives GBP Rank Here

Ran a competitive audit of top-ranking GBP profiles: review velocity — how frequently new reviews come in — turned out to be a stronger ranking driver than star rating alone. Malleshwaram outranks higher-rated competitors by generating roughly 2 reviews a day, consistently.

A separate monthly review correlated GBP service-listing completeness directly with rank recovery: branches at 83 of 85 listed services improved; Yelahanka, stalled at 15 of 85, became the weakest branch. The root cause wasn't neglect — a webinar launch and ad campaign rollout consumed nearly the entire July work calendar, leaving Yelahanka a single day and Malleshwaram zero dedicated GBP days that month.

A shared Navodaya/Sainik ranking decline was also identified across 3 branches simultaneously — reasoned as one common external cause, not three isolated problems.

Leads to Revenue — Why Cost-Per-Lead Isn't the Real Metric

Cost per lead is a useful operational number, but it doesn't tell you whether the business made money. August's data made that distinction concrete.

MetricValue
Total Leads (August)125 (87 organic, 38 paid)
Converted / Admitted43 (34.4%)

Admissions closer to exam season (July–August, largely crash-course and dropper-batch enrollments) carry meaningfully lower fees than admissions made earlier in the academic year for full-length programs, since the remaining course duration is shorter. The same admission count can represent very different revenue depending on the month — a flat lead-count or CPL comparison across months, without adjusting for program mix and timing, can be misleading. Revenue-per-admission by program type and month is the more accurate performance read.

Catching a Blind Spot in the Tracking System

Not every insight came from a dashboard. Reviewing August's lead data raised a flag: a portion of leads appeared to have no record of follow-up. The cause turned out to be structural — leads submitted through website pop-up forms route only to email, not the shared tracking sheet, since that form type also captures the exact source page (something the standard lead structure doesn't). Those emails had been landing in a spam folder undetected. Once found, all affected leads were recovered and pushed into the tracking system manually. A reminder that the system you build is only as good as the edge cases you catch.

Infrastructure Built

None of the following were requested. Built proactively because they didn't exist.

  • Leads tracking sheet — full pipeline visibility shared with the whole team
  • 18-month historical campaign analysis — corrected the internal narrative that performance had dropped
  • WhatsApp qualification filter — screens leads before the counseling team, improving conversion quality by an estimated 20–30%
  • Keyword research sheet — delivered same day as requested
  • Monthly execution calendar — shared with Rakesh for visibility
  • Geo-targeting circles — Yelahanka and Koramangala built in Google Maps for precise ad targeting
  • EOD reporting system — daily report sent every evening covering actions, movement, flags, and next day focus

Key Learnings

  • Audit before you optimise — The internal numbers looked bad because the comparison was wrong. Building the 18-month analysis first meant every decision was based on accurate data
  • Qualification matters more than volume — Removing unqualified leads from the pipeline is worth more than adding more leads. The counseling team's time is the real constraint
  • Speed beats perfection under deadline — The crash course campaign had to move same day. Copy in-house, creatives approved, landing page live in 48 hours
  • Tracking is the foundation — The Google Ads account had been spending with zero conversion signal for weeks. You cannot optimise what you cannot measure
  • Local SEO compounds fast — One month of focused GBP + location page work moved Malleshwaram from invisible to #2. The other 3 branches follow the same playbook
  • CPL means nothing without revenue context — The same lead volume and CPL can represent very different business outcomes depending on program mix and timing in the academic year
  • Review velocity beats star rating — A finding from direct competitive GBP analysis, not assumption
  • Structural content gaps hide as ranking problems — A missing page type can look like an SEO failure until you check the site's architecture, not just its rankings
  • Build systems, then audit your own blind spots — The lead management system worked as designed, but a form-type edge case still slipped through; catching it required actively reviewing outcomes, not just trusting the dashboard
